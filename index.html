Track record

Before publishing, the same model was run as of the eve of every Six Nations from 2019 to 2026, using only results and rankings available before each opening match, with parameters fixed in advance on pre-2019 data. Across 120 matches the blend had the lowest log loss (0.516, vs 0.520 for rankings alone, 0.531 for our ratings alone and 0.563 for World Rugby’s simple formula), so it is the published version. It did not win everything: it beat the World Rugby formula on matches in 6 of 8 years and on picking the champion in only 4 of 8, and in the three years with usable bookmaker odds (2020, 2021, 2024) the bookmakers scored slightly better on the champion by log loss, though the model did better by Brier score.

Method

Team strength blends two estimates with equal weight: our own ratings, built from every men's international since 2000 (recent matches count more, and teams with few matches are pulled toward average), and World Rugby's official ranking points converted to an expected margin. The home team gets a Six Nations home advantage of 4.3 points, estimated from 2004–2018 Six Nations matches. Match margins and tries are simulated with random variation, then the table is settled with the official rules: 4 points for a win, 2 for a draw, a try bonus point for 4 or more tries, a losing bonus point for losing by 7 or fewer, and 3 extra points for a Grand Slam. Ties are split by points difference, then tries scored. Results are through 31 Oct 2026; World Rugby rankings as published when queried on 4 Nov 2026. Inputs are official World Rugby and Six Nations Rugby data only. Model settings were fixed on pre-2019 data before any backtest was scored (parameters e42afbf5…).

To confirm
  • Kick-off times as published on 4 Nov 2026; they may change for broadcast.
  • Not modelled: squad selection, injuries, travel, and the November 2026 Tests still to be played (the forecast will move after them).
  • Exact ties on points, points difference and tries are ‘placed equally’ under the rules; the model shares such places at random (rare).
  • Bookmaker odds from before the opener were not found for 2019, 2022, 2025 and 2026; 2023 has only the top three prices, so it is not scored.
  • 2020 (four matches postponed to October by COVID) and 2021 (no crowds) are scored like any other year; home advantage was kept for 2021.
  • Some older World Rugby stats records omit try counts; a missing count is read as zero only when the score could be made without a try.
  • Pre-2020 ranking snapshot dates (World Rugby API ‘effective’ label unreliable).
